Read a letter

Open a letter link, pay the author’s price, and the author’s open tab reveals the key. Your payment and the reveal are both public on-chain transactions.

How it works

Seal. The author writes a short letter (max 100 characters, max 150 UTF-8 bytes so it fits in one OP_RETURN). The browser encrypts it with a fresh AES-256-GCM key and posts a transaction: OP_RETURN [ciphertext, sha256(key)] plus a 546-sat dust output to the author’s address. The key never leaves the author’s browser.

Pay. A reader pays the author’s price to the author’s address, with OP_RETURN [sha256(key)] — a public, on-chain proof of which letter they bought.

Reveal. The author’s open tab sees the payment and broadcasts a second transaction: OP_RETURN [key]. The reader verifies sha256(key) against the seal, then decrypts the letter locally. Every step is a real mainnet transaction; no server, no account, no middleman.

Limits, stated plainly. Letters are short on purpose (one OP_RETURN, max 220 bytes). The author’s tab must stay open to reveal — if it closes, the letter stays sealed until the author re-opens it with the saved key. WizardConnect signing needs a Paytaca or Cashonize wallet. Fees are about 1 sat/byte, so a full loop costs well under $1.
